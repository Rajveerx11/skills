# Roast

Attack assumptions, not the person. Produce a calibrated decision and a cheap falsifiable test—not theatrical negativity or simulated customer validation.

## Build the brief

Extract from the prompt and available context:

- customer, painful job, current alternative, buyer, and trigger;
- product mechanism and promised outcome;
- price/revenue model, acquisition channel, and delivery cost;
- founder edge, budget, timeline, and success threshold.

Ask one short batch only for missing facts that would reverse the recommendation. Otherwise state assumptions and continue.

## Run independent lenses

Analyze the same frozen brief through these lenses:

1. **Failure pre-mortem:** fatal assumptions, adoption friction, operational failure, regulatory/platform dependency.
2. **Best case:** genuine wedge, compounding advantage, expansion path, why now.
3. **First principles:** incentives, causal mechanism, constraints, and rough math without external evidence.
4. **Market evidence:** current alternatives, pricing, demand signals, switching behavior, and counterevidence from credible sources.
5. **Buyer friction:** budget owner, urgency, trust burden, implementation cost, and default “do nothing” option.

Use independent subagents when available and safe. Give each only the same brief and its lens. When unavailable, run lenses sequentially without letting earlier conclusions rewrite later premises.

The buyer lens is a hypothesis, not an interview. Label research quality and source dates. Never convert model role-play, competitor existence, waitlist signups, or compliments into proof of willingness to pay.

## Quantify uncertainty

Use ranges for price, conversion, acquisition cost, gross margin, support load, and time-to-value. Identify:

- load-bearing assumption;
- evidence for and against it;
- probability/confidence;
- what observation would change the verdict.

Do not use invented market-size precision. Prefer bottom-up reachable-market math.

## Decide

Return one verdict:

- **GO:** evidence and founder fit justify execution now.
- **RESHAPE:** a specific change preserves upside while removing a fatal flaw.
- **TEST:** uncertainty dominates and a bounded experiment can resolve it.
- **KILL:** mechanism/economics fail or evidence required is too expensive relative to upside.

Include:

- one-line call and confidence;
- strongest upside and fatal risk;
- unit-economics range and time-to-first-revenue;
- three assumptions ranked by risk;
- cheapest 48-hour test with target segment, offer, channel, sample, metric, pass/fail threshold, budget, and stop rule;
- exact next move if the test passes or fails.

Be blunt and specific. Revise the verdict if it lacks a falsifiable threshold or confuses evidence with inference.

## Adaptive excellence

- Optimize for a calibrated verdict with assumptions, economics, pre-mortem, and the cheapest decisive evidence plan.
- Use high freedom for adversarial lenses; preserve uncertainty and distinguish simulations from customers or measured demand.
- Require explicit risky assumptions, sourced economic ranges, and a falsifiable test with stop criteria. Revise once when weak.
- Learn only from real experiment outcomes and forecast calibration.
